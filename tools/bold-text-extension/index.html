What it does

Three small things that make LinkedIn posts easier to write and easier to scan.

Bold and italic

Select text in a post or comment and make it bold, italic, underlined, struck through or monospace. Press the same style again to take it off. Turn selected lines into bulleted or numbered lists.

Symbols in one click

Bullets, arrows, check marks, numbers, dividers and popular emoji. A Recent item in the right-click menu repeats whatever you used last, so repeat jobs take one click.

Side panel, toolbar and shortcuts

Click the extension icon to open a side panel that stays beside your post. A small toolbar also appears over selected text. Or use the keys you already know: Ctrl B bold, Ctrl I italic, Ctrl U underline. Press again to undo. Extra shortcuts can be changed to anything you like.

How to install and use it

It takes about a minute.

  1. Open the extension page in the Chrome Web Store and click Add to Chrome.
  2. Pin it so the icon is always visible. Click the puzzle icon in Chrome's toolbar, then the pin next to Bold and Symbols for Posts.
  3. Open LinkedIn and write your post. Select some text and press Ctrl B for bold or Ctrl I for italic (Cmd on a Mac). Press the same keys again to undo. Or right-click the selection and choose LinkedIn text style.
  4. For a bullet, arrow or check mark, right-click in the post and choose Insert symbol, or click the extension icon and open the Symbols tab.

Want extra shortcuts of your own? The extension also has Alt B and Alt I that you can change. Open chrome://extensions/shortcuts in Chrome's address bar and pick your own.

Good to know. Styled text is made of special Unicode characters, not real formatting. Screen readers may read it badly and LinkedIn search may not match it as normal words. Use it sparingly, for example for a headline or a key phrase.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no ads and no paid tier.

Does it collect my data?

No. It runs only on linkedin.com, works entirely inside your browser and sends nothing anywhere. Read the privacy policy.

Where does it work?

On LinkedIn, in desktop Chrome. Browser extensions are not available in Chrome on phones, so on mobile use the web converter, copy the result and paste it into your post.

Why does some text show as empty boxes?

The styled letters are regular Unicode characters. A few fonts or older devices do not include them, so a reader may see boxes instead of letters. This is rare on modern devices.
